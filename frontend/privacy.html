Privacy

What 8search stores — and what it does not.

This page is written to match what the 8search code actually does. It is deliberately plain: what is collected, why, how long it stays, and where it goes.

Searching without an account

You do not need an account to search. Guest searches are limited to a small daily allowance (four by default). To apply that limit, the server sets two first-party cookies: guest_id, a random identifier, and guest_searches, a counter. Both expire after 24 hours. The quota code itself does not save them to the database.

Your query is sent to third-party search and content providers so they can return results. Depending on the query, that can include DuckDuckGo, Bing, Wikipedia, Wiktionary, YouTube, Yahoo and Brave. Those providers have their own privacy policies. Results are also cached temporarily on the 8search server by query.

Accounts

  • An account stores your email address and a one-way hash of your password. The plain password is never stored.
  • A random session token is kept in an HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax cookie for up to 30 days so you stay signed in. Sessions and password-reset tokens are stored in the server database; reset tokens are hashed and expire.
  • When you are signed in, the first page of each search you make (Web, AI Answer, Images or Code) saves the query and tab in a private search history on your account so you can reopen it from the search bar. Repeating a query refreshes its place instead of creating a duplicate. Guests do not get saved history. History is visible only to the account that created it, and you can erase it at any time with History → Clear history.
  • If email delivery is configured, sign-in notices and password-reset emails are sent to your address. Those security notices can include the time, an approximate IP address, and a browser/device string.

Click statistics and suggestions

When you click a result, 8search records that the result was useful for the query. The click record can contain the query text, the result title and URL, its position, a random session identifier, a one-way hash of your account email if you are signed in, and salted hashes of the IP address and browser user agent. Raw IP addresses and raw browser strings are not stored in this database.

Click events are deleted after 90 days by default. Aggregated per-query click counts stay so ranking can keep working, and clicks decay with a 30-day half-life. Typed queries are also counted in aggregate for suggestions after passing a filter that rejects email addresses and phone numbers.

Feedback

The feedback form stores the message you write, the optional name and email you enter, your IP address, and your browser user agent. This is used to read and answer feedback and to prevent abuse. It is also emailed to the site operator when email delivery is configured. Feedback remains in the database until the operator removes it.

Photos, translation, weather and maps

  • Lens / visual search: camera frames and uploaded photos are processed in your browser and are not uploaded. Only text you explicitly choose to solve, translate or search is sent to the server.
  • Translation: text you ask to translate is forwarded to Google's public translate endpoint.
  • Weather: if you click Weather and allow location access, your browser's coordinates are used to request a forecast from Open-Meteo.
  • Maps: map tiles and earthquake data come from Esri, OpenStreetMap and the USGS.

What 8search does not do

  • It does not sell personal data.
  • It does not run advertising trackers, third-party analytics, or cross-site advertising profiles.
  • It does not store your raw IP address in the click-ranking database.

Like most web servers, the hosting environment and its logs may process IP addresses and request metadata for security and operations.

Storage, security and your choices

Account, search-history, click, feedback, suggestion and news data live in SQLite databases on the server that runs 8search. Production deployments use HTTPS, secure cookies, salted hashes and regular backups. Search history stays until you clear it or the account is removed. No system is perfectly secure, but the service is built to keep the sensitive parts hashed and the raw personal data minimal.

Clearing your browser cookies removes the guest counter and your session. Signed-in users can clear their search history from the History panel at any time. To ask about other data associated with an account, use the feedback form on the homepage.
